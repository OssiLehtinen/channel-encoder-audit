\begin{abstract}
Transformers consuming several time-aligned scalar signals (e.g.\ multivariate
sensor streams or multi-modal feature channels) typically embed each channel
independently and \emph{sum} the embeddings before feeding the result to
self-attention. We argue that this is the wrong default: summation is the
information-theoretic equivalent of broadcasting all channels onto the same
carrier, requiring the network to disentangle them downstream. We propose
\textbf{FDM channel encoding}, a frequency-division-multiplexing analog in
which each channel occupies a non-overlapping block of embedding dimensions
and amplitude-modulates a channel-specific log-spaced sinusoidal carrier, in
the spirit of rotary position embedding (RoPE). The encoding is
parameter-free. On a controlled multi-signal benchmark with non-linear lagged
interactions across channels, FDM reduces validation negative log-likelihood
(NLL) by
\textbf{0.22~nats} and raises top-1 bin accuracy by \textbf{40\%} relative
to naive sum at $C{=}4$, while using \emph{fewer} trainable parameters. We
decompose the encoding into two ingredients---per-channel block partitioning
and sinusoidal carrier modulation---and show via a third \emph{concat-only}
baseline that block partitioning alone explains $\sim$70\% of the NLL gain
and $\sim$80\% of the accuracy gain; the carrier contributes the remaining
$\sim$30\% / 20\% on top, with the marginal benefit shrinking when the
per-channel block becomes too small to host a useful frequency ladder. A
linear-probe analysis shows that FDM preserves channel identity essentially
perfectly at the input layer (per-channel $R^2 \approx 1.0$) and largely
through the network (mean $R^2 \approx 0.96$ at the final layer); concat
recovers nearly as well at the input but degrades faster through attention;
naive summation collapses recoverability to $R^2 \approx 0.24$ at every
layer. Full training, probe, and plotting scripts accompany the paper
(\texttt{<repository>}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A canonical use of transformers outside language is fitting a sequence model
to several time-aligned numerical signals: multi-sensor telemetry, financial
factor stacks, biomedical waveforms, multi-modal embeddings concatenated
along time. The standard recipe inherited from vision and language
\citep{vaswani2017attention} maps each scalar input to a vector and \emph{adds}
those vectors together---the same trick used to inject positional
information. We will refer to this as the \emph{sum} encoding.

Sum encoding has a structural problem when the input is multiple
\emph{simultaneous} channels rather than a single token-plus-position pair:
it is provably non-invertible. Given $h = \sum_k W_k x_k$, the network
cannot recover individual $x_k$ from $h$ without learning to implicitly
allocate orthogonal subspaces---a constraint that nothing in the loss
function explicitly enforces.

We borrow a fix from analog communications. \emph{Frequency-division
multiplexing (FDM)} solves the same problem by transmitting each channel on
a distinct carrier frequency, so that a receiver can demultiplex with a
bank of bandpass filters. Translated to embeddings:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Reserve a non-overlapping block of $d_{\text{block}} = d_{\text{model}}/C$
      dimensions per channel.
\item Within each block, lay down a sinusoidal carrier with a
      channel-specific log-spaced frequency $\omega_k$ and a
      rotary-position-embedding (RoPE)-style \citep{su2021roformer}
      per-dimension frequency ladder.
\item Use the scalar signal value $v_k(t)$ as an \emph{amplitude modulation}
      of that carrier.
\item Concatenate the $C$ blocks into the final $d_{\text{model}}$-dim
      embedding.
\end{enumerate}
The encoding is fully parameter-free; the only design choices are the
carrier ladder $\{\omega_k\}$ and the per-block frequency base.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A simple, parameter-free input encoding for multi-signal transformer
      inputs that is invertible by construction.
\item A controlled benchmark with non-linear lagged cross-channel
      interactions, designed so that channel identity matters.
\item Empirical evidence that FDM beats naive sum on a categorical
      generative head by a wide margin, retains its lead as distractor
      channels are added, is linearly probeable for the raw signals at
      every layer, and degrades cleanly under test-time channel masking.
\item A three-way \textsc{sum}/\textsc{concat}/\textsc{fdm} ablation that
      attributes $\sim$70--80\% of the gain to block partitioning alone and
      the remainder to the sinusoidal carrier, clarifying which ingredient
      matters for which mechanism.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem setup}
We are given a sequence $\{x_t\}_{t=1}^T$ where $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^C$ is
a vector of $C$ scalar channels at time $t$. We want a transformer to model
$p(y_t \mid x_{\le t-1})$ where $y_t$ is a downstream target. The question
we address is purely about how $x_t$ enters the residual stream.

\subsection{FDM channel encoding}
For channel $k \in \{0,\dots,C-1\}$ at time $t$, define the per-block
embedding $\mathbf{e}_k(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{block}}}$ as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_k(t)_{2i}   = v_k(t)\,\sin\!\left(\omega_k\, t\, b^{-2i/d_{\text{block}}}\right), \quad
  \mathbf{e}_k(t)_{2i+1} = v_k(t)\,\cos\!\left(\omega_k\, t\, b^{-2i/d_{\text{block}}}\right),
  \label{eq:fdm}
\end{equation}
for $i = 0,\dots,d_{\text{block}}/2 - 1$, where $b{=}10000$ is the standard
RoPE base and $v_k(t)$ is the (normalized) signal value. The full
embedding is the concatenation
$\mathbf{h}(t) = [\mathbf{e}_0(t)\,\Vert\,\mathbf{e}_1(t)\,\Vert\,\cdots\,\Vert\,\mathbf{e}_{C-1}(t)]
\in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}$.
The carriers $\{\omega_k\}$ are log-spaced across $[\omega_{\min},
\omega_{\max}]$; we use $[0.5, 8.0]$ throughout.

\paragraph{Why this is invertible.}
For fixed $t$, the per-channel block is a linear map $v_k \mapsto v_k\,\phi_k$
where $\phi_k = [\sin(\cdot)\,\Vert\,\cos(\cdot)]$ is a known unit-norm
template. Recovery is the inner product
$v_k(t) = \langle \mathbf{e}_k(t),\,\phi_k(t)\rangle$, exactly as a matched
filter demodulates an FDM channel. For sum encoding
$\mathbf{h} = \sum_k W_k v_k$ the problem is underdetermined whenever
$\dim(W_k) < \sum_k \dim(W_k)$, i.e.\ always.

\subsection{Concat ablation: block partitioning without the carrier}
\label{sec:concat}

To isolate the contribution of the sinusoidal carrier from that of the
block partitioning itself, we define a third encoder, \textsc{concat}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_k(t) = W_k\, v_k(t) + b_k, \qquad
  \mathbf{h}(t) = [\mathbf{e}_0(t)\,\Vert\cdots\Vert\,\mathbf{e}_{C-1}(t)] + \mathbf{p}(t),
  \label{eq:concat}
\end{equation}
with $W_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{block}}}$, $b_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{block}}}$
learned per channel and $\mathbf{p}(t)$ the standard sinusoidal positional
encoding. \textsc{concat} preserves channel identity (the embedding is
linearly invertible to $v_k$) but does not bake position into the channel
block, and the per-channel ``direction'' is constant across $t$. The gap
\textsc{fdm}\,$-$\,\textsc{concat} therefore measures what the carrier
contributes \emph{beyond} block partitioning---namely, an implicit
RoPE-style relative-position encoding internal to each channel, so that
attention can read off lag-dependent intra-channel structure via dot
products without learning the demix from the additive position channel.

\subsection{Sum baseline}
The baseline encoder is
$\mathbf{h}(t) = \sum_k (W v_k(t) + e_k) + \mathbf{p}(t)$
with shared scalar projection $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times 1}$,
learned channel embedding $e_k$, and standard sinusoidal positions
$\mathbf{p}(t)$. It has $d_{\text{model}}(C{+}1)$ trainable encoder
parameters; FDM has zero. For the configurations used below the gap is
${\le}\,0.3\%$ of the total model size.

We note that strengthening \textsc{sum} with per-channel projections
$W_k v_k$ does not close the fundamental gap: the sum
$\mathbf{h} = \sum_k W_k v_k$ is a rank-1 image in each $v_k$ and is
underdetermined whenever $C \cdot \dim(v_k) > \dim(\mathbf{h})$, as here.
Channel identifiability comes from the partition, not the projection.

\paragraph{Alternative: channel-as-token.}
One could instead feed each (channel, time) pair as its own token, making
the input length $C \cdot T$ and letting attention handle channel vs.\
time dependencies uniformly. This costs $\mathcal{O}(C^2 T^2)$ attention
per layer versus $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ for the encodings considered here, and
it loses the inductive bias that channels are simultaneous rather than
sequential. Block-partitioned encodings (\textsc{concat}, \textsc{fdm})
can be read as an amortization of that larger attention into a single
$T$-length stream.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
We synthesize $N{=}512$ time series of length $T{=}160$ with $C$ channels
each. Each channel is an independent stochastic process: a sum of three
sinusoids with random frequencies in $[0.005, 0.08]$ cycles/sample
(angular $\approx [0.03, 0.50]\,\text{rad/sample}$), random phases, and
first-order autoregressive [AR(1)] Gaussian noise; signals are then
standardized. \emph{The signal
frequencies do not overlap with the FDM carrier band} $[0.5, 8.0]$\,rad/sample,
so FDM cannot be winning through accidental resonance between the signal
content and its carrier. The outcome is a fixed
non-linear function of the \emph{first four} channels with two different
lags,
\[
y_t = \tanh\!\big(s_0(t{-}3)\,s_1(t)\big)
      + 0.6\sin\!\big(1.3\,s_2(t{-}7)\big)
      + 0.4\,\mathbb{1}[s_3(t)>0]\,s_0(t).
\]
Any additional channels ($k \ge 4$) are \emph{distractors}: independent of
$y$. The continuous outcome is binned into $K=32$ quantile bins so the
model emits a categorical distribution at every step.

\paragraph{Architecture.}
A small causal transformer encoder with $d_{\text{model}}=64$, 4 heads, 3
layers, feed-forward network (FFN) width 256, dropout $0.1$, pre-LayerNorm. AdamW,
lr~$3{\times}10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 32, gradient
clip 1.0, 12 epochs. Each result is averaged over 3 random seeds with
independent data re-generation.

\paragraph{Generative head.}
We bin the continuous outcome into $K{=}32$ equal-frequency quantile bins
and use a linear map to $K$ logits trained with next-step cross-entropy.
Equivalently, the head parameterizes $p(y_t \mid x_{\le t-1})$ as a
categorical distribution; sampling is trivial and NLL is a proper scoring
rule without imposing a Gaussianity or unimodality assumption that a
continuous Gaussian head would require. Bin edges are fixed globally from
the pooled outcome.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Three encoders sharing the entire transformer backbone and head:
\textsc{fdm} (Eq.~\ref{eq:fdm}, no learnable parameters in the encoder),
\textsc{concat} (Eq.~\ref{eq:concat}, $C \cdot 2 d_{\text{block}}$
parameters), and \textsc{sum} (the description above,
$d_{\text{model}}(C{+}1)$ parameters).

\subsection{Predictive performance}
\label{sec:results-main}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports validation NLL and top-1 bin accuracy at
$C{=}4$. The ordering \textsc{sum} $<$ \textsc{concat} $<$ \textsc{fdm} is
consistent across all three seeds; the \textsc{fdm}\,$-$\,\textsc{concat}
NLL gap ($0.064$) exceeds $4\times$ the pooled seed std ($0.016$), and the
\textsc{concat}\,$-$\,\textsc{sum} gap ($0.155$) exceeds $10\times$.
The bulk of the improvement over \textsc{sum} comes from block partitioning
alone---\textsc{concat} closes $71\%$ of the NLL gap and $80\%$ of the
accuracy gap---and the carrier contributes a smaller-but-stable improvement
on top. Random guessing yields $\text{NLL}{=}\ln 32 \approx 3.466$ and
$\text{acc}{=}1/32 \approx 0.031$.

Adding distractor channels (Table~\ref{tab:scaling}, Figure~\ref{fig:scaling})
tests whether each encoder can isolate the four signal-bearing channels
from the noise. All three encoders lose accuracy as $C$ grows because more
channels means more noise in the input, but the \emph{gap} between
block-partitioned encodings and \textsc{sum} is preserved (and, for
accuracy, widens from $C{=}4$ to $C{=}8$). \textsc{sum}'s NLL degrades the
least in absolute terms only because it is closest to the uniform ceiling
to begin with. The \textsc{fdm}\,$-$\,\textsc{concat} gap shrinks slightly
at $C{=}16$ where $d_{\text{block}}{=}4$ leaves only two sin/cos pairs per
block---the frequency ladder is too short for the carrier to encode much
intra-channel position---but the ordering is preserved at every $C$.

\subsection{Channel identifiability via linear probing}
\label{sec:results-probe}

\input{tables/probe.tex}

We freeze a trained model and fit closed-form ridge probes
$\mathbf{h} \mapsto \hat{x}$ from a hidden state $\mathbf{h}$ at layer
$\ell$ to the raw input vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^C$, evaluating per-channel
$R^2$ on a held-out split (Table~\ref{tab:probe}). \textsc{fdm} input
embeddings are linearly invertible to within numerical precision
($R^2 \approx 1.0$), as expected from Eq.~\ref{eq:fdm}; after three
transformer layers \textsc{fdm} still admits near-perfect recovery
(mean $R^2 \approx 0.96$). \textsc{concat} is also nearly invertible at
the input ($R^2 \approx 0.997$), confirming that block partitioning alone
buys identifiability, but it degrades more sharply through attention
($R^2_{\text{ch0}}{=}0.81$ at the final layer), particularly for the most
heavily-used channel. \textsc{sum} collapses to $R^2 \approx 0.24$ even
at the input layer---a quantitative confirmation that summation is
non-invertible---and that ceiling is unchanged after attention has had a
chance to demix. The probe and predictive results are mutually consistent:
the encoder ranking by downstream accuracy matches the ranking by
end-of-network channel recoverability.

\subsection{Robustness to missing channels}
\label{sec:results-mask}

\input{tables/mask.tex}

We zero out one channel at test time and re-evaluate
(Table~\ref{tab:mask}). For both block-partitioned encoders the per-channel
accuracy drop is monotonic in the channel's prominence in the outcome
formula---ch0 (two interaction terms) produces the largest drop, then ch1,
ch2, ch3---so the ablation profile is directly interpretable. \textsc{fdm}'s
drops are uniformly sharper than \textsc{concat}'s (e.g.\ $-0.046$ vs.\
$-0.032$ for ch0), consistent with stronger preservation of channel
identity through the network. \textsc{sum}'s profile is much flatter and
includes a $+0.005$ swing under ch2-masking that is within noise;
information about any given channel is smeared across the residual stream,
so individual channel ablations are poorly localized.

\section{Discussion}

The empirical pattern is consistent with the structural claim: sum
encoding forces the transformer to spend capacity on a problem
(demultiplexing) that the encoder can solve for free. The decomposition
into \textsc{concat} and \textsc{fdm} suggests this fix has two
separable parts. \emph{Block partitioning} is doing the heavy lifting:
once each channel owns a piece of the residual stream, attention can read
and write to it independently, and most of the predictive gap closes.
The \emph{frequency carrier} is a smaller---but real and seed-stable---
contribution on top, and we conjecture two mechanisms. First, attention
inner products of two positions in the same channel block become
$v_k(t_1) v_k(t_2)\,\langle\phi(t_1),\phi(t_2)\rangle$, exposing
lag-dependent intra-channel correlations directly to the softmax. Second,
the per-block direction varies with $t$, so the residual stream's
``slot'' for channel $k$ is naturally orthogonal to itself across time,
which appears to slow the leakage we see in the \textsc{concat} probe.

The encoding has practical implications for multi-modal or multi-sensor
settings where the number of channels is moderate ($C{\sim}{4\!-\!64}$) and
the model needs to attend to channels separately before combining them.
Existing alternatives---per-modality token streams in PerceiverIO
\citep{jaegle2021perceiver}, or the modality embeddings in
mixture-of-experts (MoE) style multi-modal transformers---typically multiply sequence length or parameter
count; FDM keeps both fixed.

\paragraph{LayerNorm interaction.} Standard LayerNorm normalizes across the
full $d_{\text{model}}$ axis, mixing per-channel blocks. Our results use
plain LayerNorm and FDM still wins; we expect a per-block (group) LayerNorm
to widen the gap further.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item The benchmark is synthetic. The outcome is deliberately constructed
      to make channel identity matter; on tasks where channel identity is
      truly redundant (e.g.\ exchangeable feature sets) sum may not be
      penalized.
\item We test only modest scales ($d_{\text{model}}{=}64$, 3 layers).
      Whether the gap closes at larger scale---once the network has the
      capacity to learn implicit demultiplexing---is open.
\item FDM requires $C \mid d_{\text{model}}$. Variable-channel inputs need
      reservation of full capacity, which is wasteful for long-tail
      modality counts.
\item Carrier hyperparameters ($\omega_{\min}, \omega_{\max}$, base) were
      not tuned per task; we use fixed values. We expect the optimum to
      depend on the target sequence length and dominant signal bandwidth.
\item Only three seeds per configuration. Error bars are informative but
      our claims would benefit from a larger seed budget at scale.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{PerceiverIO.} \citet{jaegle2021perceiver} attaches explicit
Fourier features per modality to disambiguate inputs to a shared latent
attention bottleneck. FDM differs in that the modality identifier and the
signal value are \emph{multiplied} (amplitude modulation) rather than
concatenated, and dimensions are physically partitioned across channels
rather than across feature axes.

\paragraph{Rotary position embedding (RoPE).} \citet{su2021roformer} encodes positional information
with rotation by frequency-graded angles. We re-use the same per-dimension
frequency ladder but apply it within each channel's block, with the
\emph{channel} carrier setting the outer scale.

\paragraph{TimesNet and frequency-domain encoders.}
\citet{wu2023timesnet} processes time-series in the frequency domain with
an explicit Fast Fourier Transform (FFT). Our work is dual: we perform an
inverse-FFT-like \emph{encoding} of a single value onto a per-channel frequency, rather
than transforming the signal itself.

\section{Conclusion}

Summing per-channel embeddings is the multi-signal analog of broadcasting
several radio stations on the same frequency: legible only if a downstream
demodulator is willing to learn the demix. The fix is simpler than the
name suggests: just give each channel its own disjoint block of embedding
dimensions. On a controlled benchmark with lagged cross-channel
interactions, this \emph{block-partition} recipe alone captures
$\sim$70--80\% of the gap to a naive-sum baseline; adding a
channel-specific sinusoidal carrier (FDM proper) buys a further
consistent improvement. Both variants are near-drop-in replacements
for the default embedding, preserve sequence length, and---in the FDM
case---add zero trainable parameters. We encourage trying the block
partition whenever a transformer takes more than one numerical signal at
a time, and the full FDM encoding when sequence length is long enough for
lag-dependent intra-channel attention to pay off.
